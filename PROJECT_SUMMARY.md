# Project Summary: Agreement-Weighted Self-Training for Cross-Center Polyp Segmentation with Frozen DINOv2

> **TL;DR** (8 lines)
>
> We study cross-center polyp segmentation using frozen DINOv2 ViT-S/14 features with a lightweight decoder (~535K params).
> Standard label-free adaptation methods (channel standardization, CORAL, AdaBN, TENT) all hurt performance — controlled experiments confirm correct implementation.
> The single largest improvement comes from extracting features at 448x448 instead of 224x224: +0.020 to +0.108 Dice across 6 unseen centers.
> On top of the 448 baseline, agreement-weighted pseudo-label self-training adds +0.002 to +0.027 Dice on 5–6/6 sites.
> The agreement-weighting principle (two decoders must agree for a pseudo-label to receive high weight) is established in the literature (co-training, cross pseudo-supervision).
> Our contribution is a controlled study applying it to frozen DINOv2 features for cross-center polyp segmentation.
> The adaptation gain is small compared to the resolution gain; whether it justifies the added complexity is an open question.
> All numbers below are verified from CSVs in `outputs/` and can be regenerated by `scripts/make_summary_tables.py`.

## 1. The Problem

Colorectal polyps are precursors to colorectal cancer. Automated segmentation from endoscopy images helps clinicians detect and remove them. The challenge: a model trained on one hospital's images performs poorly on images from other hospitals due to differences in cameras, lighting, patient demographics, and polyp characteristics.

We study this using frozen DINOv2 ViT-S/14 (~21M parameters, the smallest DINOv2 variant), a vision transformer pretrained by Meta on 142M images via self-supervised learning. We freeze the backbone (no fine-tuning) and train only a lightweight PatchDecoder (~535K trainable parameters: three Conv2d-BatchNorm2d-ReLU-Upsample blocks plus a 1x1 head) on Kvasir-SEG (1000 images from one center). We then evaluate on 6 unseen PolypGen centers (C1–C6, totaling 1536 images).

**Dataset composition:**

| Site | Total images | Empty masks (no polyp) | Positive images |
|---|---|---|---|
| C1 | 256 | 5 | 251 |
| C2 | 301 | 31 | 270 |
| C3 | 456 | 1 | 455 |
| C4 | 227 | 81 | 146 |
| C5 | 208 | 2 | 206 |
| C6 | 88 | 5 | 83 |

**The cross-center performance drop is severe** (positives-only Dice at 224x224, mean over 5 seeds):

| Site | Decoder Dice (224) | Drop from Kvasir val (0.839) |
|---|---|---|

The goal: **improve segmentation on unseen hospitals without labeled target data** (source-free domain adaptation).

## 2. What We Tried That Does Not Work

### 2.1 Standard Domain Adaptation Methods

We tested four established label-free adaptation methods. All of them **hurt** performance on every target site:

| Method | What it does | Mean delta (6 sites) |
|---|---|---|
| Channel standardization | Match per-channel mean/std to source | -0.018 |
| CORAL (Sun et al., 2016) | Match full covariance matrix to source | -0.025 |
| AdaBN (Li et al., 2017) | Recompute BatchNorm running stats from target | -0.036 |
| TENT (Wang et al., 2021) | Minimize prediction entropy via BN params | -0.032 |

*Channel standardization, CORAL, AdaBN numbers are from `outputs/06_poc/` Phase 2 scripts (single seed). TENT number computed from `scripts/poc_tent.py` output (single seed 42).*

We hypothesize that these methods assume the cross-center gap is a marginal distributional shift that can be fixed by matching feature statistics, but the real gap involves structural differences (camera optics, polyp size distributions, imaging protocols) that statistical alignment cannot address.

**TENT implementation details** (for reproducibility): We swept steps in {1, 3, 5, 10, 20} x learning rate in {1e-4, 5e-4, 1e-3, 5e-3} on Kvasir validation. Best configuration: steps=1, lr=1e-4. TENT optimizes only the weight and bias of the decoder's 3 BatchNorm2d layers via entropy minimization (6 parameter tensors total). Batch size=32, Adam optimizer, using the full unlabeled target set. Even the best configuration hurts on all 6 sites (deltas: C1 -0.020, C2 -0.031, C3 -0.021, C4 -0.036, C5 -0.050, C6 -0.037). *Source: `scripts/poc_tent.py` output, single seed 42, 224 features, evaluated at 128x128 decoder resolution.*

### 2.2 Prior-Matched Alignment

The source training set has ~15% foreground (polyp) patches, while target sites have 5–10%. We hypothesized that mismatched class priors were contaminating alignment statistics. We recomputed source statistics weighted by the estimated target prior. This reduced the damage but **never produced a positive result** on any site.: numbers from `outputs/06_poc/`.

### 2.3 DINOv2 Attention Maps

We tested whether DINOv2's self-attention maps could serve as a saliency prior for pseudo-label generation. The [CLS] attention from the last layer had very low overlap with polyp masks (Dice 0.12–0.25 across sites). Attention-weighted pseudo-labels performed slightly worse than unweighted ones (-0.003 Dice on Kvasir val). DINOv2 attention does not naturally localize polyps. *Source: `scripts/poc_attention_quality.py`, single seed.*

## 3. Control Experiments

Before accepting the negative results, we ran thorough controls to rule out bugs and confounds:

### 3.1 Positive Control (Synthetic Shift)
We applied a known synthetic shift to Kvasir features and verified alignment methods could recover the loss:
- **Per-channel scale+offset**: channel_std recovers 101%, CORAL 99%, AdaBN 91%
- **Mild cross-channel mixing (cond=3.2)**: 86–92% recovery
- **Aggressive mixing (cond=491)**: only 11–14% recovery

**Conclusion**: The methods work correctly on the class of shifts they are designed for.: numbers from `outputs/08_controls/`.

### 3.2 FOV Analysis
Some endoscopy images have black borders (field-of-view artifacts). We recomputed alignment statistics using only patches with >=50% FOV coverage. Result: negligible difference. The gap is not caused by FOV artifacts.: from `outputs/08_controls/`.

### 3.3 Size Analysis
We binned images by polyp area fraction and checked if the cross-center gap persists within matched bins. It does. However, we identified that small polyp prevalence differs dramatically: Kvasir has 2% of polyps smaller than 2x2 patches, vs C4 (25%) and C5 (32%). This is a major structural confound that statistical alignment cannot fix.: from `outputs/08_controls/`.

### 3.4 Resolution Validation
We confirmed that evaluating at 128x128 (decoder output size) vs original mask resolution produces no differences > 0.01 Dice, validating all prior results evaluated at decoder resolution.: from `outputs/08_controls/`.

## 4. Largest Lever: Higher Input Resolution

Extracting DINOv2 features at 448x448 instead of 224x224 gives a 32x32 patch grid (vs 16x16), allowing the model to see finer details. **This is the single largest improvement in the project, exceeding any adaptation method.**

Positives-only Dice, mean over 5 seeds (seeds 42–46):

| Site | 224 Dice | 448 Dice | Delta |
|---|---|---|---|
| Kvasir val | 0.839 | 0.871 | +0.031 |
| C1 | 0.715 | 0.735 | +0.020 |
| C2 | 0.706 | 0.736 | +0.030 |
| C3 | 0.765 | 0.808 | +0.043 |
| C4 | 0.435 | 0.478 | +0.043 |
| C5 | 0.464 | 0.572 | +0.108 |
| C6 | 0.696 | 0.756 | +0.059 |

*Source: `outputs/08_controls/ctrl_448_comparison.csv`, regenerated by `scripts/make_summary_tables.py` Table 1.*

This improvement (+0.020 to +0.108) is larger than anything the adaptation methods achieve on top of the 448 baseline (+0.002 to +0.027). Higher resolution particularly helps on C4 and C5, which have the most small polyps.

## 5. Proposed Method: Agreement-Weighted Self-Training

### 5.1 Core Idea

Since standard alignment fails, we take a different approach: instead of aligning feature statistics, we use the model's own predictions to adapt the decoder on unlabeled target data.

The core idea: **two independent decoders agreeing on a prediction is a stronger confidence signal than one decoder being confident alone.** We use this pixel-level agreement to weight pseudo-labels for self-training.

**Note on novelty**: The principle of using agreement between independently trained models to weight pseudo-labels is well-established in the literature (co-training: Blum & Mitchell 1998; cross pseudo-supervision: Chen et al. 2021). Our contribution is a controlled study applying this principle to frozen DINOv2 features for cross-center polyp segmentation, not a new method per se.

### 5.2 Method Steps

This is an **offline, source-free domain adaptation** procedure (not single-pass test-time adaptation). It uses all available unlabeled target images from a site and fine-tunes for multiple steps.

1. **Train two decoders on source data** — we tested two configurations:
   - *Cross-resolution*: one decoder at 224x224, one at 448x448
   - *Same-resolution*: two 448 decoders with different random seeds

2. **Predict on all unlabeled target images** with both decoders

3. **Compute pixel-level agreement** — where both decoders predict the same class (polyp or background), mark as "agreed". Agreement is computed by bilinear upsampling of probabilities to a common resolution, then thresholding at 0.5.

4. **Generate weighted pseudo-labels** — agreed pixels get weight 1.0, disagreed pixels get weight 0.1

5. **Fine-tune the 448 decoder** on these weighted pseudo-labels for 50 steps (BCE + Dice loss, Adam, lr=1e-3, batch_size=32)

No labeled target data is used. The method requires access to the full unlabeled target set (not a single image at a time).

### 5.6 PoC vs Ablation Discrepancy

The PoC script (`scripts/poc_multires_5seeds.py`) and the ablation script (`scripts/ablation_multires.py`) produce different numbers for the same variants because they evaluate at different resolutions:

- **PoC**: evaluates at decoder output resolution (128x128 for 224 features, 256x256 for 448 features). Uses nearest-neighbor interpolation on binary masks for agreement computation.
- **Ablation**: evaluates at original GT mask resolution using bilinear upsampling of probabilities before thresholding. Uses bilinear interpolation on probabilities for agreement.

The ablation numbers (Tables 2–6 above) are the official results. The PoC was a proof-of-concept; the ablation is the rigorous evaluation.

## 7. What We Can and Cannot Claim

**Can claim:**
- Standard label-free adaptation methods (channel standardization, CORAL, AdaBN, TENT) do not help for cross-center polyp segmentation with frozen DINOv2 features, and controlled experiments confirm correct implementation.
- Extracting DINOv2 features at 448x448 instead of 224x224 provides the largest improvement (+0.020 to +0.108 Dice) for free (no adaptation needed, just higher-resolution input).
- Agreement-weighted pseudo-label self-training improves over the 448 baseline on 5–6/6 sites and over uniform self-training on 4–6/6 sites, depending on the variant.

**Cannot claim:**
- That the adaptation gains are large: they range from +0.002 to +0.027 Dice, which is small relative to the resolution gain and to the remaining cross-center gap.
- That this is a novel method: the agreement-weighting principle is established (co-training, cross pseudo-supervision). Our contribution is the controlled study.
- That results generalize beyond this specific setting (frozen DINOv2 ViT-S/14, PatchDecoder, Kvasir-SEG source, PolypGen target).
- That the method is strictly test-time adaptation: it requires access to all target images from a site and multiple optimization steps (offline source-free adaptation).
- That K>2 decoders would help (not tested — TODO).

## 8. Limitations

1. **Single source dataset**: Kvasir-SEG (1000 images, 1 center). Results may not hold with a multi-center source.
2. **Single backbone**: DINOv2 ViT-S/14 only. Larger ViT variants or other foundation models not tested.
3. **Small adaptation gains**: The largest adaptation delta (+0.027) is small compared to the remaining cross-center gap (C4 still at 0.502 vs Kvasir 0.871). Resolution change (+0.108 on C5) is a much bigger lever.
4. **No comparison with state-of-the-art TTA methods**: SHOT, CoTTA not yet implemented.
5. **No comparison with PolypDINO**: Their repository is empty as of project start; they freeze DINOv2 and add LoRA layers + Mask2Former decoder (~0.83% trainable parameters), a fundamentally different architecture.
6. **Few-label baseline uses 224 features**: Not directly comparable to the 448-based proposed method.
7. **Empty-mask handling**: C4 has 81/227 empty masks (36%). High FP rates on empty images (53–67%) inflate all-images metrics differently per site.
8. **5 seeds only**: Standard deviations are sometimes large relative to the mean delta (e.g., C5 agree_224_448: -0.0026 +/- 0.0131).
9. **TENT and alignment evaluated at 224 only**: These negative results are at 224 resolution. They might differ at 448 (not tested).

## 9. Novelty and Contribution

**What is not new** (established techniques we build on):
- Using agreement between two models to filter/weight pseudo-labels: co-training (Blum & Mitchell, 1998), Cross Pseudo Supervision (Chen et al., CVPR 2021), n-CPS (Filipiak et al., 2021)
- Source-free domain adaptation for polyp segmentation: RPANet (Wang & Chen, IPMI 2023) already does SFDA for polyps with calibrated pseudo-labels from dual predictions
- Agreement-based domain adaptation: SImpAl (Venkat et al., NeurIPS 2020) uses classifier agreement for multi-source DA
- Frozen foundation model features + lightweight decoders: many adapter/readout papers (DINO-MVR, 2026; From SAM to DINOv2, WACV 2026)
- Confidence-weighted pseudo-labels for segmentation: extensive literature

**What is new** (our specific contribution):
1. **Controlled negative results with frozen DINOv2 features**: We show that four standard label-free adaptation methods (channel standardization, CORAL, AdaBN, TENT) all degrade performance in this setting, backed by positive controls and thorough sanity checks. This is a useful empirical finding — prior work has not tested these methods specifically with frozen DINOv2 patch features for polyp segmentation.
2. **Resolution as the dominant factor**: We quantify that simply increasing DINOv2 input resolution from 224 to 448 yields +0.020 to +0.108 Dice — larger than any adaptation method. This practical finding is underexplored in the frozen-backbone polyp segmentation context.
3. **Multi-resolution agreement as a natural byproduct**: When two decoders at different resolutions (224 vs 448) are already available, their agreement provides a free confidence signal for pseudo-label weighting. The multi-resolution angle is a minor but specific differentiator from standard CPS (which uses same-architecture networks).

**Honest framing**: This is an empirical study applying established techniques (CPS-style agreement, SFDA pseudo-labeling, frozen foundation model features) to a specific and practical problem (cross-center polyp segmentation). The contribution is the controlled experimental methodology and the findings, not a fundamentally new algorithm.

## 10. How We Frame the Research

**Title**: Agreement-Weighted Self-Training for Source-Free Adaptation of Frozen DINOv2 in Cross-Center Polyp Segmentation

**Three honest findings:**

1. **Negative finding (well-controlled)**: Standard label-free adaptation methods (feature alignment, batch norm adaptation, entropy minimization) all degrade performance on cross-center polyp segmentation with frozen DINOv2 features. Controlled experiments confirm the methods are correctly implemented but the cross-center gap appears to involve structural differences that these methods cannot address.

2. **Resolution is the biggest lever**: Extracting features at 448x448 instead of 224x224 yields +0.020 to +0.108 Dice improvement with zero adaptation, particularly for sites with small polyps (C4, C5). This dominates any adaptation gain.

3. **Agreement-weighted self-training gives a small additional gain**: Using pixel-level agreement between two independently trained decoders to weight pseudo-labels for self-training improves over the 448 baseline on 5–6/6 sites, with mean delta +0.013 to +0.015 Dice depending on the variant. The gains are real but modest relative to the resolution gain and to the remaining performance gap.

**Course topic fit**: Source-Free Domain Adaptation + Medical Image Segmentation + Transfer Learning.
